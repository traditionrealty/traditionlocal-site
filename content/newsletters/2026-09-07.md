The original Pappasito’s Cantina, the one that started it all at Richmond Avenue and Hillcroft, will close for good on Sunday, September 13, ending a 43 year run that began in 1983. Staff affectionately called it “Sito’s number 1.” Pappas has said the decision comes down to the long term outlook for the location, and every employee is being offered a spot at another Pappas restaurant. It is a real loss for a lot of Houston families who grew up on the fajitas there, and a reminder that even the most established names in this city’s dining scene aren’t guaranteed forever.

## Restaurant of the Week: Terry Black’s Barbecue

![Photo of the restaurant of the week](https://static.wixstatic.com/media/d2d48b_4a70dc2ea6aa489c818ede86139e11f8~mv2.png)

**Terry Black’s Barbecue,** 1329 N. Shepherd Drive in the Heights. This Lockhart born barbecue name opened its first Houston location on August 18, and it is already a solid family friendly stop for lunch or dinner. Order at the counter for smoked brisket, beef ribs, pork ribs, turkey, and house made sausage, plus fresh daily sides like mac and cheese, creamed corn, and cornbread muffins.

Open daily. Sunday to Thursday, 10:30 AM to 9:30 PM. Friday and Saturday, 10:30 AM to 10:00 PM..

## Market Pulse: Real Estate Update

Mortgage rates ticked up again last week. Freddie Mac’s Primary Mortgage Market Survey put the 30 year fixed rate at 6.71 percent, up from 6.66 percent the week before, with the 15 year fixed at 6.04 percent. Locally, the Houston Association of Realtors’ most recent report shows a market that is normalizing rather than heating up: median single family home price held close to flat at $340,000, up just 0.6 percent from a year earlier, while active listings hit a record 40,750, the most ever recorded here. Homes are taking a bit longer to sell, 53 days on average versus 50 a year ago, and August activity moving faster at 39 days.

Translation for buyers and sellers in our neighborhoods: more choices, more negotiating room, and less urgency than the last few years.

## Things to Do

1. **Japan Festival Houston.** Saturday, Sept 12 and Sunday, Sept 13, 10am to 6pm at Hermann Park. Free general admission, with taiko drumming, martial arts and dance performances, and Japanese food vendors. Some activities like tea ceremony and flower arrangement carry a small fee.

2. **Việt Cultural Fest.** Saturday, Sept 12th at NRG Center. Free admission. Pickleball, chess, art contests, traditional dress competitions, an artist alley, and Vietnamese food throughout the day.

3. **Houston’s Chicken Wing Festival.** Sunday, Sept 13th, 11am to 6pm at The Water Works in Buffalo Bayou Park. A live wing cook off, tastings from local restaurants, and live music. Tickets required.

4. **Franki Star ceramics exhibition.** Monday, Sept 8th through Sunday, Sept 13th at Vince in River Oaks. Free to browse, with an artist meet and greet Wednesday, Sept 9th from 5 to 8pm and 25 percent off the fall collection that evening.

**Family Pick: Children’s Museum Houston Second Thursday Free Family Night.** Thursday, Sept 10th, 5 to 8pm. Free admission for the whole family, with hands on activities across the museum floor.

**Pet Pick: Community Pack Walk with H.E.A.R.T. and Houston Humane Society.** Tuesday, Sept 8th, 5 to 5:50pm. Free. Bring your own leash or borrow an adoptable shelter dog and walk alongside other neighbors and their pets.

## Greater Houston: Bellaire Remembers September 11 on Its 25th Anniversary

This Friday, September 11, at 8:30am, Bellaire will mark 25 years since the September 11 attacks with a remembrance ceremony at the World Trade Center Beam memorial, located between the Bellaire Fire Station and City Library at 5101 Jessamine Street.

Mayor Gus Pappas, Fire Chief Davis Farquhar, and other speakers will honor both the lives lost and the first responders, survivors, and families connected to that day. The steel beam on display came from the World Trade Center itself and was donated to the city in 2017. It’s a small, quiet ceremony, but a meaningful one if you have a few minutes this Friday morning.

## Openings & Closings

Two long running Houston area spots said goodbye recently. El Tiempo Cantina’s Webster location, at 20237 Gulf Freeway, closed on September 3 after nine years, with the owners citing a hard fight to keep the doors open and hinting at a possible future comeback. Fuzzy’s Taco Shop quietly shut its three remaining Greater Houston locations in Katy, Kingwood, and Sugar Land over Labor Day weekend, part of a broader pullback for the chain across the country.

On the opening side, chef Aaron Bludorn is bringing a second Bar Bludorn to The Woodlands Waterway, set to open September 26 at 25 Waterway Avenue with the same neighborhood tavern feel as his original Memorial location. And downtown, GreenStreet has a new restaurant called Lila’s on the way at 1201 Fannin, expected sometime this fall.
