Killen’s Steakhouse in Pearland reopened September 10 after owner Ronnie Killen put a million dollars of his own money into a three month renovation of his flagship restaurant. The goal is not subtle. After two Bib Gourmand nods for his other restaurants, Killen wants this one to finally land a Michelin star. It is the kind of bet that only a chef with two decades of Pearland loyalty behind him would make, and a reminder that Houston’s suburbs are still where some of its biggest culinary swings happen.

## Food & Drink: Houston’s Latin Restaurant Weeks

Houston’s Latin Restaurant Weeks are underway now through September 25, with restaurants across the city offering prix fixe menus that celebrate Latin American cuisine. It is the promotion’s second year in Houston and its first expansion beyond the city, with Atlanta joining the lineup in October. The lineup this year spans a good stretch of Latin America: Casa do Brasil for Brazilian, Tex Chick Puerto Rican Eatery, La Pupusa Loca for Salvadoran, Parrilla Colombiana, Fonda Santa Rosa and Hugo’s for Mexican, and Exilio Latin Flair and Yuma on the fusion side, among others. Longtime names are in the mix too, including The Original Ninfa’s on Navigation and La Fisheria downtown. Worth building a dinner or two around this week while the specials last.

## Market Pulse: Real Estate Update

A Margaritaville themed retirement community is coming to Galveston Bay. Minto Communities and Margaritaville Holdings just broke ground on Latitude Margaritaville, a 3,500 home active adult development on 1,318 acres in Texas City, about 37 miles from downtown and a short drive from Galveston itself. Residents 55 and up will get single family homes, villas, and cottages built around a bandshell, multiple pools, a restaurant and bar, a fitness center, pickleball and bocce courts, and a dog park with its own pet spa. Doors are not expected to open until early 2028, but this is Minto’s first Texas project after selling out similar communities in Florida and South Carolina, and it is the kind of announcement that tends to change how people think about a stretch of coastline that has not gotten much attention until now.

**Mortgage News:** The Fed raised rates 0.25% on September 16, its first hike since 2023, as inflation remains elevated. Mortgage rates have also climbed, with the 30 year fixed reaching 6.95%, an 11 month high. We’ll be watching how this impacts local buyer activity.

## Things to Do

1. **Sinatra Under the Stars.** September 24, 6 to 9pm, at the Marriott Marquis Houston Downtown. Tickets from $45. A live tribute evening built around the Great American Songbook, string quartet and vocalist included, on the hotel’s rooftop overlooking downtown. Date night material, or just a good excuse to dress up on a Thursday.

2. **Tokyo X Anime and Pop Culture Festival.** September 26 and 27, 10am to 8pm both days, at Reliant Center. Two full days of cosplay, a talent show, and a vendor hall packed with import toys and art. If your kids, or you, have been asking to go to an anime convention, this is the local one, no flight required.

3. **ROCO: Eyes on the Horizon.** September 25 at Miller Outdoor Theatre. Free, first come first served seating on the hill or under cover. Houston’s chamber orchestra plays an evening program under the stars, bring a blanket, one of the better free date nights in the city.

4. **Come From Away opens at The Gordy.** Previews begin September 23, tickets from $73. The Tony winning musical about the small Newfoundland town that took in thousands of stranded travelers after September 11, it plays warm rather than heavy, and Stages’ production has been getting strong early word.

**Family Pick: Dewberry Farm’s Fall Festival,** weekends through November 8 in Brookshire, about 40 minutes west of the Loop. An 8 acre corn maze, a pumpkin patch, a Ferris wheel, pony rides, and a petting zoo, easily a half day trip if you make the drive out. Also worth a look: the Children’s Museum’s Fall Family Festival, September 26, 9am to 5pm, a good closer to town option.

**Pet Pick: Congressman Bill Archer Dog Park.** Seventeen acres at 3201 State Highway 6 North, with separate large and small dog areas, agility equipment, and dog bone shaped splash pools for the ones who like to cool off. If your dog has only ever known the same three blocks around your house, this one is worth the drive.

## Greater Houston: A Senior Services Contract Passes, and Harris County Raises Its Tax Rate

Houston City Council voted Wednesday, September 16, to approve a $6.7 million contract that keeps senior meal delivery and medical transportation services running. The vote had been in question, and the outcome affects thousands of older Houstonians who rely on those programs to stay fed and get to medical appointments.

Zooming out, Harris County Commissioners voted 3 to 1 on September 17 to raise the county’s property tax rate to 67 cents per $100 of taxable value, a 7.9 percent increase that adds a little more than $190 a year, about $16 a month, to the average homeowner’s bill starting with the county’s new fiscal year on October 1. Worth noting, that is Harris County Commissioners Court, a separate body from Houston City Council, which kept its own city tax rate flat earlier this year.

## Openings & Closings

![Photo for openings and closings](https://static.wixstatic.com/media/d2d48b_22e010f1024e408497604a5ad7e51759~mv2.png)

Openings first this week, there are a lot of them. Mack Allen’s, a 16,000 square foot fine dining steakhouse from the founder of Rouxpour, opened September 14 at 4400 Post Oak Parkway with Wagyu, fresh seafood, and a 1,000 bottle wine list.

Picos, the longtime River Oaks Mexican restaurant that closed its Upper Kirby location at the end of August after an outpouring of community support, reopened September 12 about two miles away at 3736 Westheimer.

In Cypress, Galiana’s Casa Del Tequila is soft opening this week, September 21 through 27, ahead of a full opening September 28, with upscale Mexican coastal cuisine in the Towne Lake area.

And in the Heights, chef Graham Laborde of Bernadine’s is bringing a new Gulf Coast seafood spot called Little Bird to 1815 North Durham this fall, 149 seats with an air conditioned patio.

On the closing side, Qin Dynasty, the Chinese American spot near West University that had served the neighborhood since 2001, has quietly shut down with no signage left behind. And Bistro le Cep on Westheimer closed over the summer.
