Michelin released its 2026 Texas Guide results for Houston on October 8, and for a second straight year the city’s six starred restaurants stayed exactly six: BCN Taste and Tradition, CorkScrew BBQ, Le Jardinier Houston, March, Musaafer and Tatemo. But do not let the stalled star count fool you. The Guide actually got bigger this year, adding 14 new names across its lower tiers, five Bib Gourmands and nine Recommended spots, for 53 Houston restaurants recognized in all.

The best story in the results is a small one. The Guide’s new Young Chef and Culinary Professional Award went to Max Lappe and Jacques Varon of Baso, the Heights restaurant built around live fire cooking. The two met years ago in Los Angeles, bonded over a shared love of fire and fermentation, and built Baso together after Varon, a Houston native, asked Lappe to move home with him. “We’re a Houston restaurant with a Basque philosophy,” Varon has said. “Almost 99 percent of our product touches the fire.” That is the kind of story we like to see: two chefs building something from scratch and getting recognized for it.

The star count did not move this year, but a Guide that keeps expanding here is still a vote of confidence in the city. Go eat at Baso if you have not already.

## Restaurant of the Week: Long Weekend

**Long Weekend,** 2044 E. TC Jester, in the Lazybrook and Timbergrove area near the Heights. This is a family built restaurant in the truest sense, a Western and ranch inspired concept from owners Andrew and Paige Alvis with a menu from veteran Houston chef German Mosquera, built around a wood fire grill and smoker. Beyond the dining room, there is a companion cafe for breakfast and lunch, a kids arts and crafts corner called Creative Canyon, and what the restaurant calls Houston’s largest outdoor play area, a Western themed playground set inside a 20,000 square foot yard with a stream, fire pits and a stage for live music. The cafe side keeps things casual and affordable with tacos, pancakes and breakfast sandwiches, while the dinner menu runs to an elk burger, chicken fried wagyu steak and rotisserie chicken if you want to make a night of it.

Open Tuesday to Sunday, 11AM to 10PM, Friday and Saturday until 11PM.

## Market Pulse: Real Estate Update

Developer Stonelake Capital broke ground the week of September 30 on 100 Park Place, a 17 story, roughly 250,000 square foot office tower at 4100 Westheimer Road in the Galleria area, two stories taller than the firm’s own 200 Park Place next door. The building will carry eight office floors over seven levels of parking, about 12,000 square feet of ground floor retail and a ninth floor amenity level with a terrace, conference center and fitness area, with JLL marketing the top nine floors to tenants. It is a speculative project, built without the usual preleasing commitments lined up first, and it is notable mostly for finally happening at all. Stonelake first targeted a groundbreaking back in 2023, pushed it to early 2025, put up construction fencing that December, and then nothing moved for the better part of a year before work actually started this fall. Delivery is now planned for the first quarter of 2028.

**Mortgage News:** Freddie Mac’s weekly survey put the 30 year fixed mortgage rate at 7.40 percent for the week of October 8, up from 7.28 percent the week before and the highest reading since November 2023.

## Things to Do

1. **Weezer: The Gathering, with The Shins and Silversun Pickups,** October 16, Toyota Center, doors at 7pm, tickets from $51. It is the first tour in years for The Shins, and pairing them with Weezer and Silversun Pickups makes this the closest thing to a 90s and 2000s alternative radio reunion Houston will see this fall. If that was the soundtrack to your college years, this is your night.

2. **Energy Day Festival,** October 17, 11am to 3pm, Sam Houston Park, free. Billed as Houston’s largest free STEM family festival, this one packs in hands on science from dozens of energy companies and universities, including a bike you pedal to generate electricity and a potato that gets turned into a working battery. It is a genuinely fun way to burn a Saturday morning downtown, and $25,000 in awards go out to area students and teachers at the event itself.

3. **Buffalo Bayou Mural Festival,** October 16 to 18, East End, free. More than 20 muralists, local and international, spend the weekend painting large scale pieces live at Headquarters Houston, with music and an open, walkable setup that makes it easy to watch the work happen in real time. Worth the drive over if you have not spent much time exploring the East End’s growing art scene.

4. **Artoberfest,** October 17 and 18, Postoffice Street, Galveston, $5 for adults. The Grand 1894 Opera House’s juried fine art festival takes over Galveston’s historic downtown for its 27th year, with artist booths set against the Victorian storefronts, plus food and live music both days. A nice excuse for a day trip to the island before the weather turns.

**Family Pick: ArBOOretum,** October 17, 10am to 4pm, Houston Arboretum and Nature Center, $25 in advance or $30 at the door, free under age 3. This is a bigger seasonal production than most of what runs at the Arboretum, with a birds of prey program, a snake encounter, pumpkin decorating, a trick or treat nature trail, train rides and a photo op with costumed characters. Costumes are encouraged and comfortable shoes are a good idea, since there is a lot of ground to cover.

**Pet Pick: Johnny Steele Dog Park,** downtown, inside Buffalo Bayou Park at 2929 Allen Parkway. Not a dated event this week, just a solid rotation pick if your usual spot is getting old. It is a smaller park than some of the ones out in the suburbs, with separate sides for big and small dogs and a pond for wading, but the setting right along the bayou with downtown in view makes it worth the trip even if you do not live nearby.

## Sports

The Astros’ season ended the way almost nobody saw coming: swept out of the American League Wild Card Series by the Chicago White Sox on October 1, two games to none. Houston backed into the postseason at 81 and 81, the first team in major league history to make the playoffs without a winning record, and the finish matched the strangeness of the path there. Chicago won game one 6 to 3, then closed it out 7 to 3 at Daikin Park, scoring four runs in the first inning after Houston starter Hunter Brown was pulled with just two outs recorded. Houston’s bats had a few moments anyway: Jeremy Pena, Cam Smith and Jose Altuve all hit solo homers, and Altuve’s was the 28th of his postseason career, second all time behind Manny Ramirez’s 29. Yordan Alvarez managed one hit in eight at bats across the series.

It was Houston’s ninth straight home playoff loss, which is its own kind of streak, and for Chicago it was the franchise’s first playoff series win of any kind since the 2005 World Series, sending the White Sox on to face Cleveland in the next round. For a team that has been in the championship conversation most Octobers this past decade, this was an unusually quiet way for the season to close, and it hands the front office an early start on what look like real questions this winter, Alvarez’s slump chief among them. If you had plans built around an Astros October, redirect that energy toward the Texans, who are off to a strong start of their own, or toward the Rockets, who open their season this month.

## Openings & Closings

Openings first. Houston natives Chelcie and Adam Aldred launched Haus Candy Co this fall, bringing what they are calling the city’s first Swedish style pick and mix candy cart to Houston, built around the lordagsgodis tradition of scooping your own weekly candy mix. The Garden Oaks based cart shows up at weekly pop up locations and private events around town, stocked with imported Swedish gummies, foams and chewy candies in flavors like sour cherry and banana toffee, with gluten free options and free samples before you buy.

Also new, Refresqueria soft opened its third location on October 3 at 2920 West Bay Area Boulevard in Webster, bringing the Texas City based chain’s aguas frescas, chamoy coated treats, elote and snow cones to the Bay Area for the first time, a genuinely kid friendly option if you are out that way.

**Closing:** Slice of Venice, the family run Italian pizzeria at 5275 Louetta Road in Spring, will serve its last plate on Sunday, October 18, after nearly two years in business. Owners Inna and her husband, who were inspired to open the restaurant by their travels through Italy, announced the closing on Facebook, writing that the restaurant had become financially unsustainable and that they are no longer able to keep the doors open.

Separately, Black Magic Social Club in Magnolia Park closed for good on September 30 after four years citing financial pressure of its own, though the space will not sit empty long. It reopens under new ownership as La Calavera in time for Halloween.
