More than 200 developers, architects and investors from across the country responded to the Astrodome Conservancy’s request for redevelopment ideas this fall, enough interest that the group pushed its original September 8 deadline back to September 25 just to keep up. It is the strongest outside interest the stalled landmark has drawn in years, covering everything from retail and hotel concepts to at least one proposal built around a data center, according to a local report.

The catch is the same one that has stalled the Dome for over a decade. Harris County government, which owns the 350 acre NRG Park site the Astrodome sits on, still has to sign off on whatever comes next, and county leadership has insisted any plan lean on private money rather than public dollars, after a $105 million county funded renovation was shelved earlier in her term. The conservancy’s executive director, Beth Wiedower Jackson, has called this round of interest proof the ideas are out there. Now the question is whether Harris County Commissioners Court is ready to act on one before the Dome’s roughly $400,000 a year holding cost outlasts everyone’s patience.

## Food & Drink: James Beard Chef Justin Yu Opens House of Louie

James Beard winning chef Justin Yu opened House of Louie on October 1 at 1931 Fairview in Montrose, his first new restaurant since 2019. Yu won Best Chef Southwest in 2016 for his tasting menu restaurant Oxheart and still runs Theodore Rex, a Michelin Bib Gourmand pick, but House of Louie is a more personal project. The restaurant is named for and modeled on a Chinese restaurant his aunts ran in Southern California for more than three decades, the same one where Yu worked as a dishwasher early in his career. “It was one of those places where there was a little more to it than what you expected,” Yu has said of the original.

The menu, developed with his longtime business partner and Anvil founder Bobby Heugel under their Thorough Fare restaurant group, leans French and Italian rather than Chinese: a house salad and marinated plums to start, beef tendon and yellowtail crudo, house made pastas including pici with crispy chicken skin, a French dip sandwich, and entrees like red snapper with beurre blanc, grilled duck and Texas wagyu. The restaurant opens nightly at 4:30pm for dinner, with Friday lunch and weekend brunch service still to come.

## Restaurant of the Week: Recess

![Photo of the restaurant of the week](https://static.wixstatic.com/media/d2d48b_fc5a104217f74e639bcd6f79b972f0ae~mv2.jpg)

**Recess,** 1500 Waugh Drive, on the border of Montrose and River Oaks. This one is built specifically for families: a two story space with a parent supervised play area for toddlers downstairs and a staffed, climate controlled play zone for ages 4 to 11, while the adults dine upstairs on a menu of chips and queso, flatbreads, steak frites and grilled salmon. The kids menu covers the basics, cheeseburgers, chicken bites, grilled cheese and pasta, and hourly play runs $18.50 for a first child with monthly memberships available if it becomes a regular stop.

Open daily, 9 AM to 8 PM.

## Market Pulse: Real Estate Update

Freddie Mac’s weekly survey put the average 30 year fixed mortgage rate at 7.28 percent as of October 2, a sharp 25 basis point jump from the week before and the highest level in the past year. It is a notable enough move to flag on its own this week, after the rate had already climbed from 6.95 to 7.03 percent over the two prior weeks, and it is worth watching closely if it continues into next week’s numbers.

**Around Houston:** Developers Freehill and JLL broke ground October 1 on Fairbanks Landing, a 1.64 million square foot industrial park at 9220 Fairbanks North Houston Road, with easy access to Beltway 8, US 290 and Highway 249. The project comes in two phases, the first pair of buildings totaling just over 809,000 square feet and set to finish in the third quarter of 2027 with Power Electronics already signed on as the anchor tenant, and a second pair of buildings, nearly 835,000 square feet, targeted for the fourth quarter of 2027.

## Things to Do

1. **Texas Renaissance Festival,** opening weekend, October 10 and 11, Todd Mission. The state’s biggest Renaissance faire kicks off its eight weekend run with an Oktoberfest themed opener, costume contests and a bratwurst eating competition set among the turrets and jousting grounds. If you have never made the drive out to Todd Mission, this is the weekend to start, and kids 12 and under get in free every Sunday of the run through late November.

2. **Bayou City Art Festival,** October 9 to 11, Memorial Park. One of the largest outdoor art fairs in the country sets up more than 280 artists under tents for the weekend, with original paintings, prints, jewelry and sculpture alongside live music stages and a food truck lineup. Tickets run $20 to $75, and it is worth the drive even if you are not in the market to buy, just to wander.

3. **Usher and Chris Brown,** October 10, NRG Stadium. Two of the biggest names in R&B are co headlining a stadium tour together for the first time, and Houston gets a stop with tickets starting around $69. This is the kind of booking that fills a 71,000 seat stadium on a Saturday night, easily the biggest ticket in town this week.

4. **Houston Cinema Arts Festival,** through October 11, multiple venues including the Museum of Fine Arts Houston, Rice Cinema and River Oaks Theatre. The 18th annual festival wraps up its run this weekend with an opening night film starring Mahershala Ali shot right here in Houston, plus a 30th anniversary screening of Wes Anderson’s Bottle Rocket, his feature debut. Date night material if your taste runs more art house than multiplex.

**Family Pick: Evelyn’s Park, Bellaire.** No specific event this week, just a standing favorite worth a fresh look now that the weather is turning. The five acre park tucked into Bellaire has a treehouse, open lawn, a labyrinth and plenty of shade, and it is a nice change of pace from the usual museum rotation if you are looking for somewhere low key to let the kids run.

**Pet Pick: Barktoberfest,** October 10, 2 to 6pm, Home Run Dugout in Katy. Houston Pets Alive’s biggest fundraiser of the year returns with a dog costume contest, live music and vendor booths, all free to attend. It benefits at risk pets across the city, so it is a good excuse to bring the dog and do some good at the same time.

## Openings & Closings

![Photo for openings and closings](https://static.wixstatic.com/media/d2d48b_d7df9ecf90f5493f92a91766c74ab6fa~mv2.png)

Openings first. The Branch opened October 1 on the 12th floor of the new Forme high rise at 5501 La Branch in Midtown, a wellness focused restaurant and rooftop lounge from chef Chris Williams of Lucille’s, serving a seasonal menu and raw bar across 20,000 square feet with a Harvest Table breakfast spread for $20.

Also opening October 2, Bar Bludorn brought its neighborhood tavern concept to The Woodlands Waterway, a $2.5 million build out of the former Baker Street Pub space at 25 Waterway Avenue from chef Aaron Bludorn’s hospitality group, mirroring the menu and feel of his original Memorial area location with country ham beignets and a short rib Reuben.

**Closing.** Early this month we noted that the original Pappasitos at 6445 Richmond Avenue, at Richmond and Hillcroft, served its last plate on September 13 after 43 years. We wanted to give you more of the story. Known to regulars as Sito’s Number One, it was opened in 1983 by brothers Chris, Harris and Greg Pappas and was the flagship of what became a citywide Tex Mex institution. The company says the decision came down to the long term outlook for that particular location, all staff are being offered positions at other Pappas restaurants, and the company is pointing longtime customers to Little Pappasitos on Kirby or Pappasitos Cantina at Memorial City in the meantime.
