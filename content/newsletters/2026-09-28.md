Trill Burgers opened its fourth Houston area location Friday, September 25, on Westheimer at Hillcroft, inside a converted Boston Market building, in what Bun B calls the hardest store the brand has ever had to open. A full kitchen remodel, new equipment, and in Bun B’s own words, considerable time and expense. Part of the difficulty was the chain’s first full drive through, a wraparound lane running until 3am on weekend nights once October hits, built for the neighborhood’s late night crowd. The halal beef spot started as a 2021 pop up, broke out after winning Good Morning America’s national burger competition in 2022, and has grown to four Houston area locations plus concessions at Rice University and the football stadium, with Dallas and Port Arthur next.

## Restaurant of the Week: Tapestry

![Photo of the restaurant of the week](https://static.wixstatic.com/media/d2d48b_7b1673e1c4ca46bb9f406ce7e8a25e0d~mv2.png)

**Tapestry,** inside the Ismaili Center in Montrose. It is an all day cafe, breakfast tacos and acai bowls in the morning, flatbreads and chicken koobideh and biryani for lunch, and a dinner menu Friday and Saturday nights. Casual, easy to bring kids to, and a nice change of pace if you are used to the same few brunch spots in that part of town.

Open Tuesday to Sunday, closed on Mondays.

## Market Pulse: Real Estate Update

Eli Lilly broke ground September 21 on a $6.5 billion manufacturing plant at Generation Park near Lake Houston, one of the largest single investments the region has seen in years. The 236 acre facility will produce the company’s new oral medication for weight loss and diabetes care, along with other therapeutics, with operations expected to start in 2030. The project is projected to generate 4,000 construction jobs and hundreds of permanent positions, and Lilly is putting $15 million toward workforce training and scholarships through San Jacinto College.

**Economic:** Mortgage rates climbed again this week, with Freddie Mac’s 30 year average rising to 7.03%, up from 6.95% the week before and well above the 6.30% rate from a year ago. It is the highest level in months, coming just over a week after the Federal Reserve’s quarter point rate hike, so if you are house hunting right now, financing costs have moved against you a bit since summer.

## Things to Do

1. **60th Annual Houston Greek Festival,** Annunciation Greek Orthodox Cathedral, 3511 Yoakum Blvd, October 1 through 3. This one has been a Montrose institution for six decades now, and it earns the crowds every year. Expect real Greek cooking, live dancing, and cultural exhibits, with extended hours Friday and Saturday running into the evening. Tickets run $10 to $40, and if you have never made it out, this is the year to finally go.

2. **Rush, Fifty Something Tour,** Toyota Center, October 1 at 7:30pm. This is the band’s first tour since 2015, and the first time Geddy Lee and Alex Lifeson have hit the road since drummer Neil Peart’s passing in 2020, so it is a genuinely big deal for anyone who grew up on classic rock radio. They are running two different setlists a night pulled from 35 songs across their catalog.

3. **Mid Main First Thursday Block Party,** 3500 Main Street, October 1, 6 to 10pm. Billed as Houston’s longest running block party, this one mixes live music, local shopping, and street art into a low key evening that is genuinely good date night material. Admission is just $10.

4. **The Sound of Music,** Hobby Center, September 29 through October 4. The touring production marks 65 years since the musical first opened, and it is exactly the kind of show you can bring the whole family to without anyone checking their phone halfway through. Tickets start at $83, worth it for a proper night out downtown.

**Family Pick: Zoo Boo, Houston Zoo,** running September 28 through October 31. Pumpkins and fall decor take over the grounds during the day, with glowing nighttime displays mixed in as October goes on. Tickets start at $33.90, and it beats fighting the crowds at the bigger pumpkin patches if you have little ones in tow.

**Pet Pick: Millie Bush Dog Park,** 16101 Westheimer Parkway. If your dog has never been, this one has two full size swimming holes plus a smaller pond for little dogs, wash stations to rinse off before the drive home, and a running track if your pup needs the extra mileage. Free, and worth the trip out toward Katy on a cooler fall morning.

## Greater Houston: Houston Parking Tickets Will Now Go in the Mail

Houston City Council voted unanimously on September 24 to let parking enforcement officers mail citations to a vehicle owner’s registered address instead of only leaving them on the windshield. Drivers will get 40 days to pay or contest a mailed ticket, up from the current 30, and the change takes effect next week.

In other council news, Marion Spann was promoted this month to Executive Assistant Chief of the Houston Fire Department, the first Black woman to hold the role in the department’s history after 32 years of service.

## Openings & Closings

Openings first. Parkwood American Grille opened in Friendswood earlier this month with a raw bar, sushi, and a full steak menu.
