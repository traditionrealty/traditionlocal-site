The city is moving forward on a $3.2 million walking and biking trail through the Alief area, a two mile concrete path connecting Arthur Storey Park to Dairy Ashford. The path will run 10 feet wide with a concrete finish, plus access ramps, signage, and route markers built in along the way. Houston has already secured $2.4 million of the cost through state and federal funding applications, leaving the rest to the city. The catch is land. A handful of property owners along the route have not agreed to sell the ground the city needs, and officials have said they may use eminent domain if those negotiations stall. Construction is not expected to start until fall 2028, so the trail itself is one to watch rather than walk anytime soon, though Arthur Storey Park on its own end is well worth a visit if you have not been.

## Food & Drink: Houston’s Latin Restaurant Weeks

Houston’s Latin Restaurant Weeks are underway now through September 25, with restaurants across the city offering prix fixe menus that celebrate Latin American cuisine. It is the promotion’s second year in Houston and its first expansion beyond the city, with Atlanta joining the lineup in October. The lineup this year spans a good stretch of Latin America: Casa do Brasil for Brazilian, Tex Chick Puerto Rican Eatery, La Pupusa Loca for Salvadoran, Parrilla Colombiana, Fonda Santa Rosa and Hugo’s for Mexican, and Exilio Latin Flair and Yuma on the fusion side, among others. Longtime names are in the mix too, including The Original Ninfa’s on Navigation and La Fisheria downtown. Worth building a dinner or two around this week while the specials last.

## Restaurant of the Week: Angie’s Pizza

![Photo of the restaurant of the week](https://static.wixstatic.com/media/d2d48b_3015ddb313b44c24b1fb7da7477c9c4d~mv2.png)

**Angie’s Pizza,** 1002 W. 11th Street in the Heights. Open since July after years of sold out pop ups, chef Angelo Emiliani (trained under Phoenix’s legendary Chris Bianco) is now firing wood fired Neapolitan and New York style pies out of a converted 1950s building. Don’t miss the Black Gold pie, loaded with mozzarella, Texas sheep’s milk Gouda, black garlic, and porcini, or the ricotta gnocchi with sausage ragu. There’s an intimate cocktail bar tucked upstairs for date night, so this one leans more special occasion than casual family stop. Book ahead, tables are going fast.

Monday to Saturday, 5:00 to 10:00 PM, closed Sundays.

## Market Pulse: Real Estate Update

If you have been house hunting, this is starting to feel like your moment. Houston’s August housing report, out September 9 from HAR, shows a market tilting toward buyers, with the median single family home price down to $330,000 and active listings up to nearly 39,000 homes. Sales did slow, down 11.5 percent from a year ago, and homes are sitting a little longer at 54 days on average, both signs sellers are having to work harder to close a deal. The one headwind is financing.

**Mortgage News:** Freddie Mac’s latest read has the 30 year fixed rate at 6.76 percent, up from 6.71 percent a week earlier and well above where it stood a year ago. More room to negotiate on price, but less room on the rate.

## Things to Do

1. **Galveston Pirate Festival.** September 14 through 20, various locations around Galveston. Tickets start at $20. The main family day is Saturday, September 20 at Moody Gardens from 11am to 7pm, with a costume contest, a treasure hunt, live bands, a kids zone, and demonstrations from blacksmiths and sword dancers. Sunday wraps things up with tours of the historic Tall Ship Elissa and the harbor.

2. **El Grito Featuring Mariachi Herencia de México.** Tuesday, September 15, 7:30pm, Miller Outdoor Theatre. Free. The two time Latin GRAMMY nominated group, 14 young musicians representing a new bicultural generation, leads a Grito ceremony and a night of Mexican music honoring Juan Gabriel. Lawn seating is open and free, or grab a free reserved seat starting at 10am the day before the show.

3. **Houston Pumpkin Festival opens at Discovery Green.** Starting Saturday, September 19 and running through November 8. Tickets run $18 on weekdays and $28 on weekends, free for children 3 and under. Expect a pumpkin covered farmland scene downtown with a 30 foot slide, carousel, bumper boats, and a sunflower walk. Food is sold separately, and buying tickets online in advance is worth it since walk up admission can be limited on busy nights.

4. **Galveston Sandcastle Festival.** Saturday and Sunday, September 19 and 20, East Beach in Galveston. $25 for adults, $10 for kids 11 and under. More than two dozen teams compete for the Golden Bucket Award, with live music, food trucks, and art vendors lining the beach. Kids can even get in on it with sand castle building lessons offered throughout the weekend.

**Family Pick: Sticking Together Day,** a celebration of caregiver and child bonding through storytelling, singing, and hands on play, included with general admission ($21.95 online). Stick around into the evening for the museum’s free Family Night from 5 to 8pm, just bring a child along to get in at no cost.

**Pet Pick: Smither Park’s 10th Anniversary Celebration** hosted by Houston SPCA, Friday, September 19, 11am to 5pm, in Houston’s East End. Free and dog friendly. The folk art park, built entirely from donated tiles, found objects, and community mosaic work next to the Orange Show, marks a decade with a festival for the whole family, four legged members included.

## Openings & Closings

![Photo for openings and closings](https://static.wixstatic.com/media/d2d48b_9883a10032c34529b35c9a1b68e77be1~mv2.jpg)

Burger Bodega is opening and bringing its halal smashburgers and specialty shakes to Katy on September 19, its first expansion outside its original location.

Underbelly Burger, the patty focused spot from James Beard Award winning chef Chris Shepherd, serves its last burger today, September 13, after four years in Spring Branch. Also gone this month is Medium Rare, the Washington D.C. import that served a single steak entree in Houston for two years before closing.
